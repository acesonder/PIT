📊 What is a Point-in-Time (PIT) Count?

A Point-in-Time (PIT) Count is a comprehensive survey conducted on a single night to count the number of people experiencing homelessness in a specific community. This count provides crucial data that helps us understand the scope of homelessness and plan appropriate services.

Why We're Conducting This Count

The Cobourg and Northumberland community is planning a warming room for the 2026 winter season. To ensure we provide adequate support and services, we need accurate data about:

  • The number of people experiencing homelessness in our area
  • The demographics and specific needs of our unhoused neighbors
  • The types of services that would be most beneficial
  • The capacity required for the warming room and support services

What Makes This Count "High-Resolution"?

An "expensive" or high-resolution PIT questionnaire means we invest heavily in data quality, comprehensive analytics, and service matching. Unlike basic counts that only capture minimum federal requirements, our detailed assessment collects:

  • Demographic information - Age, gender identity, Indigenous status, veteran status
  • Housing history - Duration and frequency of homelessness
  • Health and wellness - Physical and mental health needs
  • Service needs - What support services would help most
  • Barriers to housing - Specific challenges preventing stable housing

Your Privacy Matters

All participation is voluntary and confidential. You can choose to:

  • Full Consent: Share your complete information with Northumberland County to receive personalized support
  • Partial Consent: Provide demographic information (age, gender) without identifying details
  • Skip Any Question: Every question is optional with "Prefer not to say" or "Skip" options

Three Assessment Options

We offer three assessment lengths to respect your time while gathering important data:

  • SHORT (6-10 minutes): Essential questions only - who, where, basic demographics
  • MEDIUM (12-20 minutes): Balanced assessment including barriers and service needs
  • HARD (25-40 minutes): Comprehensive survey for detailed service planning
